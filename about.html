ABOUT THE AUTHOR

THE PERSON BEHIND
THE HOLLOW REALM

My name is M. Rafay, and I am the writer behind The Hollow Realm, a fantasy series that began with a simple idea and slowly grew into something much larger than I originally imagined.

When I first started writing, I was not thinking about creating an enormous fictional universe. I was not thinking about ancient civilizations, mysterious realms, forgotten histories, kingdoms, wars, creatures, cosmic beings, or secrets that would take years to uncover. I was simply interested in creating a story that I would genuinely want to read myself.

That idea eventually became The Hollow Realm.

What began as a story about two boys, Eric and Jacob, living on the quiet island of Varindel gradually developed into a much larger world. The more I wrote, the more questions appeared. What was beyond the Western Sea? Why had ships disappeared? What was hidden inside the lighthouse? Who had left the strange symbols behind? And most importantly, why did the world seem to contain secrets that nobody had thought to question?

I became interested in the idea that a story does not have to reveal everything immediately. Some mysteries are more interesting when they remain mysteries. A small detail introduced in one book can become important several books later. A character who seems unimportant can turn out to have been connected to something much larger. A sentence that appears ordinary at first can have an entirely different meaning once the reader understands what was really happening.

That is one of the things I want The Hollow Realm to be known for: the feeling that there is always something happening beneath the story you are currently reading.

WHERE THE STORY BEGAN

The first book, The Distant Ship, is deliberately smaller in scale than what the series eventually becomes.

Eric and Jacob grow up on Varindel, an island surrounded by stories and warnings about the Western Sea. To the people who live there, those stories have become part of everyday life. Ships disappear. Strange things happen. People learn not to ask too many questions.

Eric and Jacob, however, are curious.

That curiosity becomes the beginning of something neither of them understands.

The Distant Ship is not simply about discovering a mysterious ship or solving one mystery. It is about the beginning of a journey. It introduces the boys, their home, the people around them, and the first pieces of a much larger puzzle.

The world expands gradually because I want the reader to experience it alongside the characters. Varindel is only one place. The world beyond it is much larger, with different cultures, kingdoms, people, histories, and conflicts. As the series continues, the story moves further away from the familiar and into places where the rules of reality themselves become uncertain.

BUILDING A WORLD

One of the most exciting parts of writing this series has been creating its world.

I do not want every location to feel like another version of the same fantasy landscape. I want different places to have their own identities, histories, cultures, creatures, dangers, and mysteries.

Some places are familiar and grounded. Others are strange enough that they may seem impossible.

The further the story travels, the more the reader begins to understand that the world is not simply a collection of kingdoms and islands. There are forces and places far beyond what ordinary people understand.

And that is where the larger mystery begins.

Some worlds are beautiful.

Some are terrifying.

Some are both.

MYSTERY, FORESHADOWING, AND THE UNKNOWN

I want readers to question things.

Not every mystery in The Hollow Realm will receive an immediate answer. Some questions may remain unanswered for an entire book, or even several books.

That is intentional.

The story is designed to reward readers who remember small details.

A symbol seen in an early chapter might become important much later. A strange character might appear for only a few moments before disappearing again. Something that seems like background information may eventually become part of the central story.

I want the reader to occasionally stop and think about something they saw earlier and realize that it may have meant something completely different.

That feeling is one of the things I enjoy most about writing.

BEYOND ERIC AND JACOB

Eric and Jacob are the heart of the main story, but the world does not exist only for them.

There are other people living their own lives, making their own decisions, fighting their own battles, and dealing with consequences that may have nothing to do with the boys.

Kings have their own ambitions. Ordinary people have their own problems. Rebels have their own reasons. Characters who appear briefly may have stories of their own that the reader does not understand yet.

The world should feel alive even when Eric and Jacob are not there.

That is something I want to develop more as the series grows.

WHAT COMES NEXT

The Distant Ship is only the beginning.

The later books expand the world, introduce new characters and conflicts, uncover older secrets, and take Eric and Jacob far beyond the life they once knew on Varindel.

But the series is not only about getting bigger.

It is about going deeper.

Every new location should reveal something. Every major character should have a reason for being there. Every mystery should connect to something larger, even if the reader does not understand the connection immediately.

There will be moments of adventure, mystery, friendship, conflict, loss, discovery, and decisions that permanently change the direction of the story.

Some answers will lead to even bigger questions.

And some questions may not be answered until much later.

WHY I WRITE

I do not write because I think every story needs to follow a particular formula.

I write because I enjoy creating worlds that do not exist anywhere else and then asking myself what would happen if real people were placed inside them.

I want readers to become attached to characters, become curious about places, remember tiny details, argue about theories, and notice things that they might have missed the first time.

Most importantly, I want the world to feel bigger than the pages containing it.

The Hollow Realm started with two friends on an island.

But the story does not end there.

There are places they have not seen, people they have not met, histories they do not know, and mysteries they have not even realized they are part of.

And that is exactly where the journey begins.
